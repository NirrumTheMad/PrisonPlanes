	I am Minaë.  I was once the god queen of a world of golden towers. My power of light was the beacon that guided my people to a righteous world. I sought the farthest reaches of the universe to shine my light.

I am now a traffic support technician. The Light I work with controls the flow of vehicle and pedestrian traffic.

Magic is thick here, on Tajik. I can work wonders while sneezing that exceed the light of the sun from which I gained my powers. But here, were darkness leaks from the stones, and half the sky has no stars, It takes effort to light up my own room. 

It is lit. I am no *novice*. I am Innumerable, above uncounted, above finite. I am respected, and people work to accomplish my ends, a sizable number of them fight daily for my attention, a sizable number of them are kept around simply to help me keep focus.

My day begins with a breakfast of ScunGees Good Blend. It is high in protein, savoury, and nourishing. It is exceedingly dull in comparison to Kōma, a food I have had once. Kōma is, to my meager knowing, a food so incredibly engineered, that had my foes back on... ah, home, ever tasted it, my empire would have crumbled and my screams would have had no one to hear them before the meal was done. It taught my body new ways to harvest nourishment. I could eat sea silt and be stronger than any of my Muuj, my strong warriors. I drink Snilk, a branded drink that is exceptionally high in fat and low in bugs, somehow, to wash down the spongey granules. A satisfying morning. 

My clothing here is simple. My nightclothes were once my finest royal regalia, and to my pride they are still my most comfortable clothes, and delightfully cheap to mend and clean, which I should probably do soon. I toss them on my bed... 

My bed is a Thraskian Mod station, a high upgrade from my old Manafactor. My Manafactor calmed and stilled my magic like I was young again. The Mod station has been altering my nerves to interface with technologies, replacing my cells with metals, the smallest spindle of which could have held up all of the golden towners. I can *hear* the song of my home star at all times. I listen to it for comfort. Comfort from the annoying notification that is in my Eye implant, an uncounted servant telling me that I am running late. I like him. I might even let him have my table scraps for free... if I can afford that. 

I don my work clothes. Light and a million curses flee from me, no longer able to reach my skin. Today, there is a junction on the south end that needs upgrading. Another notification tells me that fourteen will need repair. I send technicians out to those sites to prepare the rituals. My clothes I would call grubby, unkempt, but they are comfortable, and impenetrable to any force or vehicle my people ever dreamed of. Thick though. 

My tools are already on site. Except my once-great staff, Heyga, which I've modified to fit in my pocket as a pen, and expanded its uses. This morning I do consider that, dwelling on the memory of working hard to become who I am. I relish, just a little, just for a moment, that I can continue growing so freely, even in this... dreary and tiring land. I put my hair up into my helmet, it needs to be cut, a harder feat than ever, now that it sometimes kills things on its own accord, I shall need a better barber. 


I grab my boots. With them I can walk on vertical surfaces without thought. Their very existence dispels Unending torments from ambient hells, and pierces defences and offences that make the mighty Awsé seem trivial. I realise that *it is* trivial, as my once great foe grumbles deeply from the rooftop. My ride to work. I climb out onto the fire escape, shuddering to think what a fire in this city would even look like, and clamber onto his back. He is also ready for his own day, Armoured in custom fit braided wire, guarding the scrap zones so that the uncounted remain orderly. He drops me off at the depot. My superior, also innumerable, grumbles about wondering when I'd show up. He knows my work, knows that for these tasks, I am unmatched, which means I am always the one to do them. The depot is a swirl of ancient hexes, the likes of which I have dispelled many many times in my long life as queen, but it is still an awe to know the presence of so many curses, and not a single one aimed at me, Most of them aimed at engines or people that no longer exist. They grasp at the magic around, clawing, like a nauseating fog, like a smell. You get used to it, they told me. I haven't. I take one of the Grollers. In my world we would have flown. Flying is reserved for the rich here, The higher your status, the higher you may fly. I am allowed to drive, at least. One of my Uncounteds has the door open for me. Two more are in the back, ready to hand me tools. Slavery was forbidden on my home. Here, no one *isn't* a slave. Slaves with no master. Suddenly I am driving on roads I would have considered indestructible were it not for their ability to somehow form potholes. The Groller connects to my implants. In a fit of boredom I climb on to the roof of  the Groller Cab. Just to feel the wind. To see something other than the road in front of me. It would be a bad idea, but my magic lets my body slip around the dangling obstructions. Passing as light through the air. A God of light lives on this street, once second in his pantheon, now, not even my peer, his Shrine tucked on a street corner underneath an overpass. A small thank you to his home, and suddenly the world seems more right. The engine runs smoother, and one of my uncounteds, whose name I now remember, Ohli, finds and fixes a bolt that was loose on the shelf inside the Groller, preventing a mess. I offer a second thanks to that god, for reminding me how much this world tries to take from you. When did I begin forgetting names? I knew the names of all of my Muuj, prided myself in being able to recognise them within their armour, recognise them from their skeletons alone. Ohli and Herž, my associates. Jigo, the name of the man I intend to know better. I send him a message asking him to share a meal tonight, simultaneously upon sending it, I get a notification from him saying that his insurance contribution just stepped up into finite. I inform him the name of the street the little god's shrine is on, and to make sure to get their name. Smug bastard.

We Arrive, Ohli and Herž disembark and begin removing the plate of the Traffic cabinet, and our enemy is immediately clear. A Cryptoangel, once the great servant of a deity, now a pitiful sprite, has diluted the song of the lights, making them out of sync. Ohli, I remember why I bring them around, has already grounded my ankle. Herž offers a Vide, a path to the heavens contained in a tube with a bit of circuitry attached, with a bucket at the other end. A vacuum. I don't need it. I take Heyga from my pocket and peel the offending celestial, name and form, from the circuitry. I note that it came in through the elech, which is sealing shut as I work. It wasn't working alone. Herž has taken notice and has already sent a notification to dispatch in my name. I wouldn't've noticed that on another day, of course they do, they have to work harder than anyone in order to even eat. I begin to work, Heyga, once used to compose sparkling citadels now repairs this chip, whose grinning AI spins back into health.

We shut the panel and begin to move. The next three jobs are much the same, and I begin to suspect something is afoot before the fourth. Something or someone is targeting the traffic cabs. I make the call to dispatch. I can't get through. Someone is targetting me. I almost breathe a sigh of relief. Something to shake up the eternal dwale of this place. We begin the fourth job. I don't have time to be assassinated, nor do I want people to know that I know. 

I thank Herž and Ohli, by name, when we enter the Groller. I know what's coming next. I can feel their names slipping from me already. A spike of light lances toward me from below the overpass, through the window without touching it. The fools. Light? I am Minaë, God Queen of Hålša, Even in this dark world. The beam gets pulled into Heyga, which I have now perched within my mouth implants, held by machine parts as I drive. My light now. I yell to the others to get low and clamber out onto the roof. It takes a strong gravity to pull me down, but this cursed world has other forces that wish me grounded. We drive off of our route, where six Cryptoangels assume battle forms. Stormy beasts with bird faces and wide hands. I vaguely recognise them, Interesting. Lances of electricity strike the groller, Ohli dies quickly, Herž is simply paralysed. A shame. He won't last long. Oh well, not much holding me back now. I put down my visor and hold Heyga in my thickly gloved hand. I bind the first of the three lowest angels to the whisper of a shadow of a streetlight. It won't get out any time soon. The other two prepare to charge. Another laser targetting beam lights my right shoulder. When the laser fires I am there to grab it, and there to pierce one of the greater angels with it, dispatching one more of the lesser with the stored beam. As we pass a sign I see behind the streetlights, a golden arc of nova flare. No chance for Herž now. The three remaining angels sense it too. The Last lesser skewers my stomach with its lightning lance. I don't flinch as I stare down its betters. They move to flee from the flare. I capture one within the shadow of my glove, and the other I mark with light. I'll find it later.

The announcement comes. "Incoming Nova Flare, Seek Deep Shelter."

My Groller turns to hide behind the largest building I can find. I ram it through a closing garage door. I do not want to get dinged for losing the tools. 

"Incoming Nova Flare, Seek Deep Shelter."
My uncounted isn't going to survive. But I am impressed to see him try. He crawls toward the closed door, which I graciously blast the handle off  before I begin my ascent. The dying rays of the mortal star are powerful to even me. I leave my coat in the Groller. It is harder than it should be, to fly. It feels like pushing against a soft bed that doesn't stop sinking, but doesn't let you go fast.

"Incoming Nova Flare, Seek Deep Shelter."

Heyga readies to absorb the incoming cosmic rays as I limp above the tops of the tallest buildings and descending barges, dismantling from its compact pen into the greater array I've built it into. Others have joined me in the sky, those few geared to harness the power of a dead sun. The city goes eerily Quiet. The few screams of the uncounted barely audible in the high winds. It has been so long since I was allowed this high. No one. Not the Nombari, Not the corporations, no heaven or hell denies us this gift. 

The Solar winds blast into the city, occasionally slowing on pockets of frozen time, somehow missed by resanding efforts. Arrays from DWE begin to sweep over the city to blunt the blow and harvest some of the power. Micrometeors pelt me like snow. My power waxes. electromagnetic eddies attempt to fry my new parts, but I am Minaë, Sun-scorched Queen of...an old home. 

The blast hits me full, and sends me flying across the sky, along with many of the others.  It is over in a moment. I land softly after I regain my balance. A quick sip of the stored sunlight and I am back to check on the Groller. The inside of the Garage is scorched, but machines are already working to rebuild the transport. Behind the broken door, huddled atop a door, is the remains of my uncounted. I recover his uniform and toss it in the back, and go to continue my job. It takes almost an hour for the city to get back up to speed. In which time I have completed my job rapidly. 

I speed back to the depot, trying to race the upwelling traffic, which flows easily enough. My van and tools are checked over, and two new uncounted are getting trained... by Jigo, who raises his eyebrows a few times at me. Maybe today is a good day. I've got one name to remember. 

I give my report. I have a trapped cryptoangel in my coat. My superior is rather suddenly not in the room, but a Squat, squished creature, in a colourful and expensive robe. I finish my report, my suspicions of tampering, and my superior, suddenly returned to the room as if nothing had happened, nods, and says "we'll take care of it"

My insurance contribution has gone up by half in the last ten minutes, not enough to exceed the rank of innumerable but I am tantalizingly close. 

I head out again, this time in a smaller Jōbug, Upgrading a junction is easy work. Take out the old part, put in the new one, keep the old one for recycling. I've forgotten to have lunch again today. Probably for the best. This junction now is aligned with the Local Traffic Controller, which received its major upgrade a couple of years ago. Now it can slow down time for speeders, bringing them to the speed limit of their station. Some Vehicles are exceptionally capable of warding off this effect. This is as designed, they paid the price for that. Outside of the cities, some of them reach astonishing speeds. Some of them can't even really be considered fast so much as *elsewhere-er*. The new part I installed can throw down about 6 bubbles at a time, so no one really needs to *stop*, and there's plenty of time to react if slowing is needed. An amazing invention I wish I could bring back to my people... back...home.

I close the cabinet, it lowers into the street again. Done. Plenty of time remains in the work day but I've not gotten word of any more work to do! Clearly, I must do my duty and inspect the traffic from... that café window. I love coffee. We didn't have any on my world. It makes my teeth tingle. I think often about returning home, and I will one day, to gather Shesi seeds and check to see if there are any of the bright fish remaining, I could sell them to some company, make a bit more of a name, a bit more sand. I almost relish the idea of returning home, the God-queen returned with impossible power back to a place where a bed is just a soft spot to lie down and food is just something your body turns into more body. I wouldn't be able to stay. Too much work to do here. As I down my second cup and a grey BisGut-Cinnamish™ A notification update appears in my eye. 

"I've got the traffic on this intersection covered, you bring the Jōbug back"

I look around to see my superior, sitting down with a coffee of his own, He flexes one claw from his many-fingered hand, his people's equivalent of a wink. I flash him a wink of my own, and it takes him a moment to recognise the gesture and return a nod. I finish my coffee and snack, and do as he says. Leisure is to be seized in this world, robbed from the cracks of ever-tightening bureaucracy by ever-climbing efficiency and cheating. I park the Jōbug in the dispatch lot, where uncounteds swarm to put away the parts within its small compartment.  Jigo is nowhere to be seen, his workday has ended, apparently. I'll have to hurry.

A good day, only a few have died, and a smug little god has earned himself a perch on my windowsill. Jigo has earned himself a meal beyond his standing and *maybe* I'll have someone else to talk to, other than grumpy old Awsé, whose Mighty wings, now wet and black with gore announce the end of his own shift with their thunderous beats. The blood is fried from existence as I let a little sunlight out, and we head homeward.

What I do not see is the squat little creature holding on to my jacket, grasping the cryptoangel tight inside its meaty tendril. I do not see it watching me as I depart. I don't see it release the angel, and in particular, I do not see it disappear from sight.

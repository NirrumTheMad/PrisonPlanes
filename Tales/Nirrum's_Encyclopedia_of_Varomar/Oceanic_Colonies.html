Snarl is an oceanic plane, the lone continent a relatively minor break in the water-shielded surface. Those dry folk think their world is the center of snarl but The center of a bubble is rarely the most interesting part.

From the cold Northernmost and southernmost currents, to the nearly intolerable coastal gulfs, the oceans are teeming with life.

The regions are harder to differentiate, perhaps, than the continent. Much of the ocean is the bacterial mat, feeding on the marine snow that plummets from the surface constantly. Much of the mesopelagic is open, trackless water. The surface changes constantly of course. Each passing breeze combining with every swell to make a unique part of the song described by the waves.


The Sea is a horrid place. It is governed exclusively by the conflicts of the immensely strong. Krakens, Draconids, Elementals, and other things more comfortable in the deep vie for power against eachother like gulls over a sandwich.


Principal among the more sane deep-things are the Whales, who know more about divinity than even most gods. For the best, as the great war of the sea is between them and the Krakens, though some might argue that both sides are the same. Literally

With their birth-granted magic, Whales are immensely powerful druids, capable of changing shape more or less at will, changing ecosytems on a whim, or causing devastation that requires work to understand. Krakens, conversely, turn their power inwards, to much the same effect. A kraken is intrinsically weaker than a whale because of their inability to rely on others, having sequestered their divinity for whatever cause they're attempting, which is usually ascension to godhood. Whales tend to recognise this is an antisocial behaviour, and often adventure to stop them. It is hard to say how many whales were born whales, and how many Krakens were born Krakens. While their life cycles appear wildly different, and their methods of interacting with the world are also different, there is unreasonably accurate parity between missing whales and new Krakens.
Of course, Snarl is a place of many things from many worlds so there are perhaps many oceanic titans that would recognise themselves as Krakens, but of course, newcomers to snarl are often stunned with how things work around here.

Entirely less important than these creatures are the more familiar-seeming moist civilizations, who war and fight and engage in politics as such smaller, less confined creatures do, though the truth is that they all do so at the whim of those greater powers. Their Architecture made to normalise pressure and current so that the passing fights of larger creatures don't disturb their lives. Many of the most social of these species live nearer to the coasts other the occasional oceanic archipelago, as the food there is more palatable and easier to reliably catch. When these civilizations have problems they cannot solve, they often seek the advice of one of the whales, who are quite reliable for advice.


Sea elf city brainstorming

Sea elves are oddities within the ocean. While ntirely amphibious, they build their civilizations in the deeper, cooler parts of the oceans anyway, though they stay away from its most perilous depths. Despite their depths, they are clearly descent of dryfolk, lacking many of the features that make aquatic life comfortable. They don't seem to mind. They trade with the surface Grao in deep-sea iron, and rare medicines made from the living mat of bacteria that covers the ocean floor where currents do not scour. To many creatures of the ocean, they are known as the hard-people, which has a dual meaning referring to Shell and Bone. One of the more valuable spells of their people is wall of bone, from which they make both their temporary bivouacs and greater cities. Of course this knowledge of how to manipulate bone, shell, and some forms of rock has come with both horrific wars and interesting technological revelations. On the surface above their settlements, huge bowls of bone are left floating to catch (mostly) fresh rainwater, anchored to the seafloor with nacrous chain. For Sea-elf settlements, it is a dangerous journey that must be undertaken nearly daily, as though they are significantly more salt-tolerant than most elves, they are not native to the sea, and still require fresh water. The fastest elves are always lauded and honored with the trek, as often, more powerful mages, especially clerics, cannot be spared. Water is stored in airlock traps within houses, snail-shell-shaped divots in the cieling that hold the mostly fresh water they have gathered. Life in the deep is a constant fight against parasites, some of which worm their way up from the deep as horrific new plagues. Food is never a problem, as elvish magic is extremely able to ensnare the minds of most creatures of the ocean. Song is used to lure them in to waiting harpoons, often the prey doesn't notice until long after they have been speared. The most prevalent export of the sea elf cities are nacrous crafts, Mercury pulled from their own flesh and drawn from any patron titans nearby, and various native ore exchanged with the grao for refined metals, which are much harder to forge underwater. The song-hearers use Mother of pearl mandalas as spell foci to augur the future, Each one a different tradition and understanding of the titanic songs of Kraken and Whale as they engage in their perpetual war. Each Mandala grants specific kinds of insights, and each tradition shapes those insights into certain loci, often the one that was most useful for the context of that region, as a result, sometimes leftover mandala can be rediscovered by new civilizations attempting to build a new home. Also using this technology, the sea elves can hide messages in different colours of nacre, only visible under the correct lighting, frequently with many messages overlaid. Some use this in coming-of-age traditions, in which a child is walked through a hallway at different stages, or when different lessons need to be taught, each under different light, and from different angles. This magic is also done in wildly different traditions, using the boundary between colours in ways unheard of in landling art, often weaving in temporal aspeccts that are tied to the position of the viewer. Light means more where the sea elves dwell, as it is often so dark, that there is little colour without it. Occasionaly they have nacrous Artificers who build sea-elf pearl armor
